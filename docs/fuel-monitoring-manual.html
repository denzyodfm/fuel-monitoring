01

Access

Sign in and hold your session

The system is reached on port 3010. Every page except the sign-in screen requires a session, so an expired session sends you back here rather than showing an error.

  1. Open http://192.168.4.200:3010 from any machine on the office network. You land on the sign-in screen automatically.
  2. Enter your email address and password. Both were issued by an administrator.
  3. Select Sign in. You arrive on the dashboard, and the sidebar shows only the areas your role can reach.
  4. To change your own password, open Settings in the sidebar and stay on the Account tab. Staff, approvers and viewers must confirm their existing password first; administrators are not asked for it.
  5. To finish, use the sign-out button at the top right of the header.

A session lasts eight hours. After that you are returned to the sign-in screen and simply log in again — nothing you saved is affected. Wrong credentials return the message “Invalid credentials” without saying which field was wrong.

02

Orientation

Read the operation at a glance

The dashboard is the landing page for every role. It totals your whole organisation — it is not filtered, and it excludes deleted records.

  1. The seven cards read left to right: total fuel expense, fuel volume, transaction count, average price per litre, pending approvals, active vehicles, and insurance alerts.
  2. Insurance alerts counts policies expiring within the next 90 days. Anything above zero is worth opening the Insurance page for.
  3. Monthly expense and litres plots every month on record. Expense follows the left scale in pesos; litres are drawn on the same axis as a flat comparison line.
  4. Expense by company ranks your top eight charging entities by total amount.
  5. Recent transactions lists the six newest records. Administrators and staff get Edit and Delete directly from this table.

Pending approvals counts records with the status Submitted. The approval workflow is recorded in the database, but there are no approve or reject buttons in the current release — treat this card as a count to watch, not a queue to clear.

03

Daily work

Record a fuel purchase

Administrators and staff can create records. The four fields marked • are required; everything else can be filled in later through Edit.

  1. Open Transactions in the sidebar, then select Add transaction at the top right.
  2. Set the Date • of the purchase and choose the Company • being charged.
  3. Enter the P.O. number and Invoice number from the slip. Both are free text, so keep your numbering consistent — searches match on them.
  4. Pick the Vehicle, Operator and Purpose. Only active operators and purposes appear in these lists.
  5. Enter Unit price • in pesos per litre and Litres •. Do not enter the total — the system multiplies these two for you.
  6. Add the Odometer reading if the vehicle requires one, and Requested amount if the driver asked for a different figure than what was actually pumped.
  7. Select Create transaction. You return to the transaction list with the new record at the top.

Requested versus actual. The Requested amount is what was asked for; the computed Amount is what was really dispensed. The Reports page subtracts one from the other and shows the difference as Variance, so filling this in is what makes that column meaningful.

04

Retrieval

Filter, summarise and export

The transaction list holds 25 records per page. Every filter narrows the table, the totals row, the summaries and the exports together — so whatever you export is exactly what you are looking at.

  1. Type in Search to match a P.O. number, invoice number, plate number, asset name or driver name.
  2. Narrow further with Vehicle, Driver / operator, a From and To date range, and Status.
  3. Select Filter to apply, or Clear to return to the full list.
  4. Read the Filtered totals row at the foot of the table — it totals litres and pesos across every matching record, not just the visible page.
  5. Select View summaries for subtotals grouped by company, by vehicle and by driver, each listing the P.O. numbers involved. From there you can Download PDF or Print.
  6. Use Download Excel for the filtered rows as a spreadsheet, or Print for a clean printed copy with the filters recorded at the top.
  7. Move through pages with Previous and Next beneath the table.

Status offers Draft, Submitted, Approved, Rejected and Locked. Records created in the app start as drafts and stay there, so this filter is most useful for data brought in from earlier systems.

05

Corrections

Fix a mistake, or remove a record

Editing is the normal way to correct a record and is available to administrators and staff. Deleting is deliberately harder, and only administrators can complete it.

  1. Find the record on the Transactions page, or in Recent transactions on the dashboard.
  2. Select Edit. The form opens with the current values already filled in.
  3. Change what you need and save. If you altered the unit price or litres, the amount is recalculated for you.
  4. To remove a record, select Delete and type DELETE in capitals into the confirmation box.
  5. Select Delete transaction to confirm. Anything other than exactly DELETE is refused.

Every change is logged. Creations, edits, deletions and sign-ins are written to the audit log with your name and the time, and the previous values are kept. Corrections are safe to make — but they are never anonymous.

06

Foundations

Keep the reference lists accurate

Companies, Vehicles, Operators and Purposes all behave the same way. They feed the drop-downs on the transaction form, so a missing entry here is the usual reason someone cannot record a purchase.

  1. Open the page you need from the sidebar. The table shows how many transactions already reference each row — useful before you change anything.
  2. Select Add company, Add vehicle, Add operator or Add purpose at the top right, complete the form, and save.
  3. Use Edit on any row to correct it. Setting a row to inactive keeps its history intact but removes it from the drop-downs on new transactions.
  4. On Companies, Operators and Purposes you also get a Search box with a live result count, plus Print and Download Excel.
  5. Delete asks you to type DELETE, exactly as transactions do, and is administrator-only.

What each list is for. Companies are the entities being charged. Vehicles carry the plate, assigned driver and whether an odometer reading is required. Operators are drivers and equipment users. Purposes categorise the fuel use and mark it as personal or billable.

The Vehicles page has no search box. It is the one list without the search, print and export tools, so use your browser's find (Ctrl + F) on a long fleet.

07

Oversight

Track cover and read the reports

Insurance has two views. All policies is the flat list, soonest expiry first. By vehicle groups every asset by what it is — vehicle, generator, engine, equipment or property — and shows its current cover with the renewal history behind it.

  1. Select Add policy, choose the asset, then enter the insurer, policy number, start and end dates and the premium.
  2. A policy is Expiring soon once it is within 90 days of its end date, and Expired after it. This is the same window the dashboard uses for its insurance alert card.
  3. Use Needs attention to show only policies that are expiring or already expired.
  4. Switch to By vehicle and select History on any asset to see its previous policies. Assets with no cover at all are listed too, marked No policy recorded.
  5. Edit corrects a policy; Delete asks you to type DELETE and is administrator-only. Print and Download Excel follow whichever view you are in.
  6. Open Reports for the monthly summary: month, company, litres, actual spend, requested amount, and the Variance between the last two.

Renewing? Add the new policy rather than editing the old one. The previous policy then moves into that asset's history, which is what gives you the renewal trail.

Categories are worked out from the asset name when a vehicle has no Vehicle type set — a record called “GENERATOR” is filed under Generator. If something lands in the wrong group, set its Vehicle type on the Vehicles page and it moves.

08

Settings

One place for accounts and identity

Settings holds four tabs. Account is available to everyone; Branding, Users and Audit log appear for administrators only, so most people open Settings and see a single tab.

  1. Account — change your own password here. Everyone has this tab, whatever their role.
  2. Branding — change the system name, short name and tagline that appear on the sign-in page, browser tab, sidebar and header.
  3. Under footer branding, upload a provider logo (PNG, JPG or WebP up to 2 MB) or set a short letter mark used when no logo is present. Select Save branding to apply.
  4. Users — select Add user, then set a name, email, role and a password of at least eight characters, entered twice. Leave the password blank when editing to keep the current one.
  5. Deactivate someone by clearing Active instead of deleting them; their history stays attached. The system will not let you remove your own administrator access or deactivate your own account.
  6. Audit log — the last 100 events, sign-ins, creations, edits and deletions, with the person, the record and the originating address.

Timezone, currency and upload storage are shown on the Branding tab but cannot be edited. They are fixed to Asia/Manila and the Philippine peso.

Quick reference

Three things worth remembering.

Never type a total

Enter the unit price and the litres. The amount is computed as price × litres and stored to the centavo — a typed total would disagree with every report.

Deactivate before deleting

Clearing Active removes a company, operator or purpose from new transactions while keeping its history. Deleting is for genuine mistakes only.

Filters follow you out

Whatever the filters show is exactly what Print, Download Excel and the summary PDF contain — including the totals row.

If a drop-down is empty

The entry is missing or inactive. Add it under Companies, Vehicles, Operators or Purposes, then return to the transaction form.
